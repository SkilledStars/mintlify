Talk to it like a colleague. Names, @handles and links are enough: your client never needs an id, and every answer says how it read you.

## Explore an audience

- "Who are mothers in their late 30s who care about online privacy, on YouTube?"
- "Map the home espresso audience. Who are they?"
- "Show me MrBeast's audience." A channel's name is enough; so is its @handle or a channel or video link.
- "Who buys from yourbrand.com?" A website is read for who its customers are.
- "Split that map by life stage." Or by themes, groups, or something of your own, like "price sensitivity".
- "Open the second group and split it again."
- "In the specialty coffee group, which small channels have the highest fan density?"
- "What else do these fans subscribe to?"
- "Tell me about GNCA on this map." A channel named on a map is that map's channel: how much of the audience comments there, and where else its commenters go.
- "Show me the wider landscape around them." Up to about 1,500 channels in audiences, as the page's Macro view.
- "Who sponsors these channels?" Brands that sponsor them, and the ones they link to as affiliates.
- "Which smaller creators should a brand partner with?" Hidden gems: the highest fan density among small channels.
- "Are these kids on their parents' accounts?" On maps where kids comment, it tells the kid's own viewing from a parent's.
- "Compare espresso machines and grinders on this map." Compare takes products and interests, like the app's Compare tab.
- "Only brand-safe channels, please."
- "Write an audience report and save it."
- "Save this map as Espresso creators." Then later: "Open my saved map Espresso creators."
- "Save those channels as an audience called Espresso fans."

## Build an inclusion list

- "Build an inclusion list of 200 channels for espresso gear buyers, US only, at least 25K subscribers."
- Paste a brief or an email: "Turn this into an inclusion list."
- "How much data do these seed channels have?"
- "Is it ready? Show me the top 20 and anything flagged."
- "Why is this channel on the list?"
- "Give me the Google Ads file."
- "Leave out these channels, we've already bought them." Exclusions are added to, or taken off, one by one.
- "Raise the minimum to 100K subscribers and run it again."

Downloads come as the Google Ads placements file, the full list, or a workbook. Links last 24 hours. A list run is paid, so your client asks before starting one. When a name could be several channels, the closest is taken, as in the app, and the answer names the others so you can switch.

## Work on a project

- "Start a project to understand the home espresso audience."
- "What do they say about grinders?"
- "What are they passionate about?"
- "Make the report, add a section on how often top channels post, and give me a link to share."
- "Run this report every Monday and email me what changed." Then: "What changed since last week?"
- "Which channels are behind that answer? Save them as an audience."
- "Make an inclusion list of the top 100 channels from this project."
- "Write a creator report on my channel. What do my fans want next?" Then: "Give me a PDF of it."

## Keep an eye on something

- "Every week, tell me which of these channels' fans mention our product." Your client can show you the first 30 days before it starts.
- "What did the latest run find? How does it compare with the one before?"
- "Also ask whether they mention Roblox." Or: "Stop following the giveaways theme."
- "Email me this run."

## Run campaigns

- "What's in my workspace?"
- "Create a Demand Gen campaign for my brand using the Espresso fans audience, then show me the targeting."
- "Remove anything about travel from the targeting."
- "Create it in Google Ads, then sync it."
- "What's waiting to sync on Spring launch?"
- "Was anything changed in Google Ads since the last sync? Keep their budget, and our targeting."
- "Bring in my existing Google Ads campaigns."
- "How did Spring launch do over the last 30 days, by ad group?"
- "Give me the Google Ads Editor file for the Video campaign."

Google Ads campaigns are always created paused. A sync sends settings, targeting and creative, as the app's Sync does, and your client asks before each one. Video campaigns go over with the Editor file.

## Good to know

- **Big jobs take a few minutes.** Reports and lists keep working while your client checks back. If it loses track, ask "is it ready?"
- **Several workspaces?** Fandom uses the one you used last from that app. Name another to switch.
- **People means people who comment,** not everyone who watches.
- **Your client asks before anything paid or anything that changes your account.** Maps and reports it just runs.
